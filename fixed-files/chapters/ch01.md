## Learning objectives
1. Whoever buys an option gets a right (a choice). Whoever sells it takes on a promise they must keep if asked.
2. The fee is paid at the start and the seller keeps it whatever happens. The agreed price and the end date set the terms.

## Choices
- **A. Make the promise.** Take the £8 now. If [CHARACTER_A] asks on [DATE], buy their 10 crates at £10 each.
- **B. Decline.** No fee, no promise.

## Reason prompt
Before you pick: in a sentence, what's your reason?

## Expected maths
- Fee received: 10 × £0.80 = **£8**, kept whatever happens.
- [CHARACTER_A] will only use the right if crates are worth **less** than £10 on [DATE] (otherwise they can sell elsewhere for more).
- At £12 (what happens): right not used. Result **+£8**.
- At £7 (what-if): you pay £100 for crates worth £70 = −£30, plus the £8 fee = **−£22**.
- Breakeven: £10 − £0.80 = **£9.20** a crate.
- Most you can make: **£8**. Most you can lose: **£92** (if crates became worth nothing: −£100 + £8).
- [CHARACTER_A]'s side is the mirror image: at £12 they're down the £8 fee; at £7 they're up £22.
- Decline: £0 in every case.

End-date price: **£12** a crate.

## Learn panel
**What did I just learn? A put option.**
- **Put option**: the right, not the obligation, to sell something at an agreed price by or on a set date. [CHARACTER_A] bought one.
- **Writing (selling) a put**: you took the other side, a promise to buy at the agreed price if asked.
- **Premium**: the fee for the option, paid up front. Here, £8.
- **Strike**: the agreed price. Here, £10 a crate.
- **Expiry**: the end date. After it, the right is gone.
- **Exercise / assignment**: the buyer using the right is "exercising"; the seller being made to keep the promise is "being assigned".
- Real-world note: in this story one option covers 10 crates. A standard US share option usually covers 100 shares, so real amounts are bigger. Our numbers are fictional pounds, not market prices.

## Recap questions
1. **Q:** In this chapter, who had the right, and who had the promise?
   **A:** [CHARACTER_A] had the right (they bought it). You had the promise (you sold it).
   **If wrong:** [CHARACTER_A] paid £8 for the choice to sell. A choice is a right. You were paid to stand ready to buy. That's a promise.
   *Concept: right-vs-promise*
2. **Q:** The price ended at £12. Why didn't [CHARACTER_A] use the right to sell at £10?
   **A:** They could sell elsewhere for £12, so selling to you at £10 would lose them £2 a crate.
   **If wrong:** A right is only worth using when it beats the market. £10 from you, or £12 elsewhere: they take £12.
   *Concept: put-option*
3. **Q:** If the price had ended at £7, what would your result have been?
   **A:** −£22.
   **If wrong:** You'd pay 10 × £10 = £100 for crates worth 10 × £7 = £70. That's −£30. You still keep the £8 fee: −£30 + £8 = −£22.
   *Concept: put-option*
