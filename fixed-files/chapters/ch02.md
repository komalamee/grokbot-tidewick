## Learning objectives
1. A cash-secured promise sets aside the full amount you might have to pay (units × agreed price). That money isn't free to use while the promise is open.
2. The fee you receive is not your profit yet. Your result is only known when the promise ends, and a bigger fee usually comes with more risk.

## Choices
- **A.** Promise to buy 10 barrels at £12. Take £9 now. Set aside £120.
- **B.** Promise to buy 10 barrels at £11. Take £5 now. Set aside £110.
- **C.** Decline both. Keep all £480 free.

## Expected maths
While the promise is open:
- A: cash £480 + £9 = £489. Set aside £120. **Free to use: £369.**
- B: cash £480 + £5 = £485. Set aside £110. **Free to use: £375.**
- C: £480, all free.

At the end date, £13 a barrel (what happens). Both prices are above the agreed price, so neither right is used:
- A: the £120 is released (it was always your money, so there's no second £120 coming in). Cash £489. **Result +£9.**
- B: cash £485. **Result +£5.**
- C: £480. Result £0.

What-ifs (the fee isn't the result until the end):
- At £11.50: A's right is used. You pay £120 for barrels worth £115 = −£5, plus £9 fee = **+£4**, and you now hold barrels instead of cash (cash £369, barrels worth £115). B's right isn't used: **+£5**.
- At £10: A: −£20 + £9 = **−£11**. B: −£10 + £5 = **−£5**.
- Breakevens: A **£11.10**, B **£10.50**. Most you could lose: A **£111**, B **£105** (barrels worth nothing).
- The bigger fee came with a higher agreed price: more money set aside, a higher breakeven, and bigger losses if prices fall.

## Learn panel
**What did I just learn? A cash-secured put.**
- **Cash-secured put**: selling a put while setting aside enough cash to buy the goods at the agreed price if you're assigned. Here, £120 (A) or £110 (B).
- **Premium is not profit**: the premium is yours to keep, but your result depends on where the price ends. Until then, it's a fee for carrying risk.
- **Breakeven** for a sold put: agreed price minus the fee per unit. A: £12 − £0.90 = £11.10.
- **Moneyness**: a put's agreed price above today's price is "in the money"; below is "out of the money". Both offers here start out of the money (today £12.40), B more so.
- Real-world note: brokers may also offer margin accounts, which ask for less than the full amount set aside. That's a different arrangement with different risks; this game uses full cash set aside until a later season.

## Recap questions
1. **Q:** You took Offer A. While it was open, how much of your cash was free to use?
   **A:** £369.
   **If wrong:** You had £480, plus the £9 fee = £489. £120 was set aside for the promise: £489 − £120 = £369.
   *Concept: cash-secured-put*
2. **Q:** When the promise ended unused, why didn't you get £129 back (the £120 plus the fee)?
   **A:** The £120 was always your own money. It was set aside, not spent. The fee was already in your cash from day one.
   **If wrong:** Count the cash: £480 at the start, £489 after the fee, £489 at the end. Setting money aside doesn't change your cash; it just stops you using it.
   *Concept: cash-secured-put*
3. **Q:** Offer A paid more. Was it the better offer?
   **A:** Not simply. It paid £4 more but set aside £10 more, had a higher breakeven (£11.10 v £10.50), and would have lost more if prices fell.
   **If wrong:** At £10 a barrel, A loses £11 and B loses £5. A bigger fee is payment for a bigger risk.
   *Concept: premium-not-profit*
